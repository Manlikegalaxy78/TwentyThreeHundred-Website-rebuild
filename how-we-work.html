How we work

Deliverables-based. Collaborative. Transparent.

We don't charge for turning up. Our approach is built around clear outcomes, shared accountability and a commitment to transparency that keeps every engagement on track — and keeps our clients in control.

Our approach

Four principles that govern every engagement.

These aren't aspirations — they're how we actually work, reflected in our contracts, our ways of working documentation and how we structure delivery periods with clients.

01

Delivery of Value

Our services are based on deliverables; we do not charge for simply turning up. This approach ensures we maintain a sharp focus on achieving results and guarantees value for our clients. Every engagement has clear acceptance criteria defining what "delivered" means.

02

Customer Collaboration

We collaborate with customers to establish a shared vision, evaluate current conditions, and make incremental progress towards achieving that vision. PMO+ Community Leads are invited to our daily stand-ups and regularly attend.

03

Incremental Delivery

Our approach integrates a structured discovery and delivery process with agile, value-driven releases. We plan one period ahead, review at the end of each period, and adjust based on what we learn.

04

Transparency

By working openly with our customers — sharing progress, decisions and insights — we create a collaborative environment where ideas evolve quickly and risks surface early. This enables fast, meaningful feedback and keeps us aligned.

Ways of working

How a typical engagement runs.

Period planning — In accordance with our framework approach, TwentyThreeHundred delivers increments of work over agreed periods of time. The length of a period is agreed in advance and can be varied — historically we have worked to four-week, one-month, six-week and two-month periods as deemed optimal.

Period backlog — The total "ask" for a period is recorded on the TwentyThreeHundred Jira board. Each service catalogue item requested becomes a backlog item, with the period for delivery, the catalogue item reference and the acceptance criteria.

Daily stand-up — We run a daily stand-up to monitor delivery against the period backlog. Progress is discussed, blockers are identified and issues raised. Customer representatives are invited and regularly attend.

End-of-period review — At the end of each period, TwentyThreeHundred schedules a review. All completed items are discussed to ensure stakeholders are aware of all items completed and to ensure each item meets its acceptance criteria.

Acceptance certificates — Following the review, a record of completed items is sent to relevant leads. Items are assured and any disputed items are flagged. An acceptance certificate detailing completed items is then signed by both parties.

Change management — Additions, deletions and abandoned items are identified and recorded as they are discussed and agreed. All change must be authorised by the relevant community leads and is recorded against the relevant Jira item.

Delivery cadence

From backlog to accepted — every period.

Step A

Requirements captured

PMO+ Community Leads request service catalogue items based on customer requirements for the period.

Step B

Backlog agreed

Items are recorded in the period backlog on the Jira board, costed per the service catalogue, and agreed with the contract manager.

Step C

Kanban delivery

Items move from To Do → In Progress → Done. Daily stand-ups track progress, blockers and any agreed changes.

Step D

Accepted & invoiced

End-of-period review, non-commercial acceptance by community leads, formal acceptance certificate signed and invoice raised.
